ASX ANNOUNCEMENT ALPHA · FORWARD VALIDATION TERMINAL

One signal survived the research. This terminal is where it gets its real verdict.

Over five weeks I taught an LLM pipeline to read 5,364 ASX filings — every director trade, results announcement, dividend and board notice for the ASX 200 across two years — then event-studied everything the market supposedly leaves on the table. Post-earnings drift, dividend-cut momentum, insider dip-buying, CEO changes: all null. The market prices announcement news the day it lands.

One pattern refused to die through eight robustness attacks: large ($1M+), freely-timed director sales — filed well clear of results season, where free choice lives — preceded roughly −5–6% per quarter of sector-adjusted underperformance. In-sample, small-n, mechanism unknown. That is a hypothesis, not an edge.

So it graduates to the only test that counts: a frozen, pre-registered strategy running forward on filings it has never seen, in an IBKR paper account — no real capital, ever. Confirmed or refuted, the result gets published. This page is the hub: the live equity curve and signal book the strategy generates, and below them the research library — the full study, with two further write-ups on the way. Code, build log and every negative result: github.com/Taylor-Hobbs/asx.

Do ASX Director Trades Predict Returns?

"We collected every director-interest filing for 199 ASX 200 tickers over 24 months, extracted 4,743 trades with an LLM benchmarked at 93.1% field accuracy, and tested whether director trading predicts returns. Conclusion: no identifiable edge. The naive −7.1% sales signal decomposes into three self-caught artifacts — double-counted windows, alpha extrapolation, reporting-season momentum. What survives: large, freely-timed sales at −5.7%/quarter (t = −2.2, 21/31 negative) — not earnings-mediated, not a sector artifact, and in-sample only. It is now frozen and running forward on this terminal."
